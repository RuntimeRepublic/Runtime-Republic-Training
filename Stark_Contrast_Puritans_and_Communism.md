# Stark Contrast: Puritans and Communism

*(Approximately 7 minutes when read aloud)*

One group treated a fixed standard as something elites could not rewrite. The other treated the standard itself as something elites could revise at will. The difference is not minor. It is the difference between ordered agency that survives pressure and systems that collapse under their own capture.

The Puritan measure, as recovered in the Agentic Puritan Revival thesis, is simple. Liberty in the open state is measured by the degree to which ideas, practices, and institutions escape elite capture. Elite capture happens when human authorities add, subtract, or invent binding requirements beyond what the standard itself prescribes. The Regulative Principle of Worship, stated in the Westminster Confession of Faith, Larger Catechism, and Shorter Catechism, supplies the model. The acceptable way of worshipping God is instituted by himself and limited by his revealed will. He may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or in any other way not prescribed in Scripture. God alone is Lord of the conscience. He has left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men that are contrary to his word or beside it in matters of faith or worship.

What is commanded is permitted. What is not commanded is forbidden in the regulated sphere. Silence in the standard is restrictive, not an invitation for human invention. Magistrates, clerics, and innovators possess no residual authority to bind conscience once the standard has spoken or remained silent. This is not maximal individual autonomy. It is not freedom defined by the absence of constraint. It is the refusal to let human devices quietly become the binding rule.

Under the Clarendon Code after 1660, English Puritans and Nonconformists lost official pulpits, university places, and civil offices. The established institutions had been captured. They responded by building parallel structures: daily self-examination recorded in diaries, mutual accountability in meetings, practical education, and commercial discipline. Agency increased as dependence on elite-defined rules decreased. In New England the same habit produced covenants, elected officers, and written limits that treated authority as derived rather than original. These were not perfect societies. They retained establishments and inconsistencies. Their distinctive success was the refusal to let the imaginations of men become the standard.

Communism proceeds by the opposite principle. There is no fixed, non-capturable standard. The theory is the property of the party and its leading intellectuals. What the standard requires can be revised by those who hold power. Silence is not restrictive; it is an open field for the devices of the vanguard. The result is systematic elite capture. Central planners claim the authority to invent binding requirements for production, distribution, speech, and association. Local knowledge is overridden. Self-examination is replaced by external surveillance and confession to the party. Parallel institutions are suppressed rather than tolerated. Authority is treated as original in the party, not derived from a standard the party itself cannot rewrite.

The practical outcomes diverge under pressure. When official institutions were closed to them, Puritans and Nonconformists continued to produce, trade, educate, and discipline themselves. Their commercial networks and record-keeping habits persisted because the standard did not depend on state permission. Communist systems, by contrast, have repeatedly produced shortages, famines, and coerced labor precisely when the party’s devices replaced any fixed measure of what may be done. The same central authority that rewrites the standard also concentrates the power to enforce the rewrite. Dissent is not merely disagreement; it is an attack on the captured idea itself.

The modern extension of the Puritan pattern treats control documents as the fixed standard. Tools, AI assistants, and collaborative workflows remain subordinate to it. Lessons teach; the standard rules. Success is measured by whether participants can act, detect deviation, and correct course without waiting for elite permission or accepting unauthorized additions. Elite capture is escaped when no human authority can silently rewrite the standard or treat its silence as a license to invent binding requirements.

Communism offers no such escape. The standard is whatever the current leadership declares it to be. The Agentic Puritan Revival tests whether the older pattern—fixed standard, continuous accountability, voluntary association, and refusal of elite devices—can again produce ordered liberty that does not depend on the imaginations of those who hold power. The contrast is stark because one side treats the standard as non-capturable and the other treats capture of the standard as the ordinary means of rule. The difference is not abstract. It is the difference between systems that build capacity under exclusion and systems that require exclusion to maintain the capture.
