# Agentic Puritan Revival

## One-Page Thesis

An Agentic Puritan Revival recovers the Puritan practice of ordered agency under a fixed, revealed standard that cannot be captured by elites. Liberty in the open state is measured by the degree to which ideas, practices, and institutions escape elite capture. Elite capture occurs when human authorities add, subtract, or invent binding requirements beyond what the standard itself prescribes. The Regulative Principle of Worship, stated in the Westminster Confession of Faith, Larger Catechism, and Shorter Catechism, supplies the model.

Westminster Confession of Faith 21.1 declares that “the acceptable way of worshipping the true God is instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representation, or any other way not prescribed in the holy Scripture.” Confession 20.2 adds that God alone is Lord of the conscience and has left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men “which are in any thing contrary to his word, or beside it, in matters of faith or worship.” The Shorter Catechism (Q. 50–51) and Larger Catechism require receiving, observing, and keeping pure all religious worship and ordinances appointed in the Word, while forbidding any other way not appointed, including additions invented by men or received by tradition under any pretence.

This is not a humanist or classical-liberal definition of liberty. It is not maximal individual autonomy, non-aggression as the primary rule, or freedom defined by the absence of external constraint. Those frames are set aside. The Puritan measure is whether a practice or institution remains limited to what the standard authorizes. What is commanded is permitted; what is not commanded is forbidden in the regulated sphere. Silence in the standard is restrictive, not permissive. Human elites—whether magistrates, clerics, or innovators—possess no residual authority to bind conscience by their own devices once the standard has spoken or remained silent.

Puritans applied this most rigorously to worship and church government, refusing will-worship and unauthorized impositions. The same logic produced resistance to civil requirements that treated human inventions as binding on conscience. Under the Clarendon Code they built parallel structures of self-examination, mutual accountability, practical education, and commercial discipline precisely because official institutions had been captured. Agency increased as dependence on elite-defined rules decreased. In New England the same habit produced covenants, elected officers, and written limits that treated authority as derived rather than original.

The modern form extends the principle to open systems. Control documents function as the fixed standard. Tools, AI assistants, and collaborative workflows remain subordinate to it. Lessons teach; the standard rules. Success is measured by whether participants can act, detect deviation, and correct course without waiting for elite permission or accepting unauthorized additions. Elite capture is escaped when no human authority can silently rewrite the standard or treat its silence as a license to invent binding requirements.

The claim is practical. Puritans were not consistent on every point, and New England retained establishments. Their distinctive success lay in treating a revealed standard as non-capturable and in building capacity under that constraint. An Agentic Puritan Revival tests whether the same pattern—fixed standard, continuous accountability, voluntary association, and refusal of elite devices—can again produce ordered liberty that does not depend on the imaginations of those who hold power.

## Key Westminster Sources

- **Westminster Confession of Faith 21.1** (Of Religious Worship): The acceptable way of worshipping the true God is instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representation, or any other way not prescribed in the holy Scripture.
- **Westminster Confession of Faith 20.2** (Of Christian Liberty, and Liberty of Conscience): God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men which are in any thing contrary to his word, or beside it, in matters of faith or worship.
- **Shorter Catechism Q. 50**: The second commandment requireth the receiving, observing, and keeping pure and entire, all such religious worship and ordinances as God hath appointed in his word.
- **Shorter Catechism Q. 51**: The second commandment forbiddeth the worshipping of God by images, or any other way not appointed in his word.
- **Larger Catechism** (on the Second Commandment): Forbids all superstitious devices, corrupting the worship of God, adding to it, or taking from it, whether invented and taken up of ourselves, or received by tradition from others.

## Relation to Runtime Republic / AGCL

Control documents remain the authority. Training materials (lessons, essays) teach the pattern; they do not invent or expand the standard. The measure of liberty is escape from elite capture of the standard itself.
