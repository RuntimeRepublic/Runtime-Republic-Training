# 1. Purpose

Defines how Runtime Republic explains *why* the AGCL is built the way it is, through three
lenses: a first-principles review, a design rationale, and political theology. Lessons that
teach foundations follow this document.

---

# 2. Core Principle

**Scripture is the chief control; the AGCL applies it; witnesses agree with it.** The order,
highest first, is Scripture, the canonical AGCL documents, this repository's CONTROL
documents, then witnesses (CONTROL 01 §6; AGCL Control 000, proposed). A foundation explains
how a rule applies Scripture. It never changes a rule by itself: a suspected conflict is
flagged and resolved by the Human Engineer's Ruling and, where needed, amendment under AGCL
Control 00J (AGCL Control 000, Rules S2–S3).

# 3. Model / Rules

## 3.1 The three lenses

| Lens | Question it answers | Audience | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-principles review | What is the idea, in plain words? | Any citizen | One or two sentences per principle |
| Design rationale | Why this rule, and not another? | Engineers, office holders | One record per AGCL control or Point |
| Political theology | How does the rule apply Scripture? | Readers who want the moral basis | Classified Scripture links |

Each foundation is recorded through all three lenses, so the same idea can be taught to a
citizen, defended to an engineer, and grounded for a believer without changing its meaning.

## 3.2 First-principles review

1. Each principle is one or two short sentences in plain words, readable aloud (CONTROL 05 §6).
2. Each principle names the AGCL control or Point it explains.
3. A principle never states anything the canonical AGCL documents do not support.

## 3.3 Design rationale

4. Each record answers five questions, in this order:
   a. **Problem:** what failure does the rule prevent?
   b. **Rule:** which AGCL control or Point, and what it requires.
   c. **Mechanism:** why this mechanism was chosen.
   d. **Alternative:** the obvious alternative and why it was rejected.
   e. **Check:** how anyone can tell the rule is working.
5. The rationale is recorded separately from the rule. Changing a rationale never changes
   the rule; changing the rule requires the canonical AGCL repository.

## 3.4 Political theology

6. Every Scripture link carries exactly one classification:

| Class | Meaning | Example wording |
|---|---|---|
| Command | The text directly commands this | "Scripture commands…" |
| Principle | The text states a principle the rule applies | "Scripture teaches…" |
| Precedent | The text records an example the rule follows | "Scripture shows…" |
| Analogy | The text governs something else; the rule reasons by comparison | "By analogy…" |
| Modern design | No scriptural basis; an engineering choice | "The AGCL adds…" |

7. A link is never described with stronger wording than its class allows.
8. Every link that has a counter-text names it (a passage that limits, qualifies or cuts
   against the claim).
9. Scripture is quoted from the King James Version.
10. A rule with no scriptural basis is classified Modern design, never stretched to fit.
11. **Whole-system citation.** A practice drawn from Scripture is cited with its conditions:
    its purpose, the consent by which it was adopted, its limits, and its reset. A tax cited
    without its method of approval and who consented is returned in review (AGCL Control 000,
    Rule S4). Israel's civil laws are cited for their "general equity" as a whole system
    (Westminster Confession 19.4), never as single provisions pulled loose. Supporting texts:
    Exodus 24:3, 7 (the law adopted by the people's consent); 2 Samuel 5:3 (kingship by
    covenant with the elders); Deuteronomy 17:14–20 (limits on the king). AGCL Control 00E
    Rule E1 is the modern form: no obligation without a declared purpose, payer, duration
    and sunset.
12. **Witnesses.** Economists, historians and theologians are cited as witnesses who agree,
    never as authorities (AGCL Control 000, Rule S5).
13. **Rulings.** Where a reading is disputed, the lesson cites the Human Engineer's Ruling,
    issued by the documented hermeneutical method (AGCL Control 000, Rule S3 and §VI). Until a
    Ruling exists, the lesson marks the reading as pending.

## 3.5 Foundations Register

Starting entries (2026-11-04). New entries are added only with Human Engineer approval.

### F1 — Nothing exists simply because it exists

| Lens | Entry |
|---|---|
| First principle | Every authority and every tax must earn its next term. When its purpose is met or its time runs out, it ends unless people deliberately renew it. |
| Design rationale | **Problem:** temporary authority and taxes become permanent by default. **Rule:** AGCL Control 00A: "No authority may exist by inertia. No program may survive its purpose." Authority includes every fiscal power, so taxes are covered. It expires automatically (A1), renewal needs affirmative action (A2), and unmet metrics end it early (A3). The same rule runs through debt and spending: every debt has a sunset (00E), thresholds trigger automatic funding reduction (00F), and debt and spending must decline until debt is zero (Points 05.1, 05.3, 09). **Mechanism:** a timer plus metrics forces Plan-Do-Check-Act: Plan (defined problem, duration, metrics), Do (act within the window), Check (A3), Act (A2 renew, or expire). **Alternative:** open-ended authority with repeal by vote; rejected because the burden falls on those who want it ended. **Check:** every authority shows its expiration date and metrics publicly. |
| Political theology | Exodus 36:3–7 — collection stopped once the need was met (**Precedent**; a voluntary offering, not a tax). Leviticus 25:10–16 — land priced by years left to the Jubilee, so a claim shrank to zero (**Analogy**; governs land, not taxes). Deuteronomy 15:1–2, 12 — debts and servants released every seventh year (**Analogy**). Genesis 41:34–36 with 47:24–26 — Joseph's one-fifth, tied to seven years, became permanent (**Precedent**, cautionary). Automatic expiration, metrics and decline schedules are **Modern design**. Lessons teach the rule; specific rates and schedules belong to the canonical AGCL documents. |

### F2 — Ownership is protected from the state

| Lens | Entry |
|---|---|
| First principle | Ownership is absolute against the state; the owner is a steward before God. What you own cannot be taken by recurring government claims, and nothing may be pooled before its owner is known. |
| Design rationale | **Problem:** government can take what people own through recurring claims, or pool it before anyone knows whose it is. **Rule:** AGCL Control 00C: "Ownership precedes authority" and "pooling without ownership is confiscation." Point 09 ends federal authority to tax property: no recurring claims on land or assets, and no transfer of ownership through taxation. **Mechanism:** ownership is declared before anything is pooled, value reverts to its owner when a program ends or fails, and no recurring tax can attach to owned property. **Alternative:** recurring property taxes softened by exemptions and relief; rejected because the owner never fully owns, and title can still pass for unpaid tax. **Check:** no asset is pooled without a declared owner, and no title passes for an unpaid recurring tax. |
| Political theology | Exodus 20:15, 17 — no stealing or coveting (**Principle**). 1 Kings 21, Naboth's vineyard (**Precedent**): the owner refused the king on God's law (21:3), and even the king had to ask and offer value (21:2); the taking required a counterfeit legal process of a fast, false witnesses and a staged trial (21:8–13), which is why the AGCL requires open records (Control 00G) and published text before action (One-Hour Rule); God corrected the ruler through Elijah (21:19; 2 Kings 9:26). Israel's revenue never charged the land itself (**Precedent**): the tithe was on increase (Leviticus 27:30; Deuteronomy 14:22–29), the sanctuary half-shekel was flat (Exodus 30:11–16), and land returned to its family at the Jubilee (Leviticus 25:10–13). Genesis 47:20–26 (**Precedent**, cautionary): Egypt's recurring fifth began only after the people sold their land to Pharaoh, so it was rent to a ruler who had become the owner. Stewardship: Leviticus 25:23 (the land is God's) and 19:9–10 (gleanings) are duties the owner owes to God, never claims a ruler may collect; the owner left the gleanings, the king did not take them (**Principle**; pending a Ruling under AGCL Control 000 Rule S3). |

### F3 — Taxation is legitimate but bounded

| Lens | Entry |
|---|---|
| First principle | Government may tax, but it must show why each tax continues. |
| Design rationale | **Problem:** taxes attach to income and labor, hide inside prices, continue by inertia, and get spent outside lawful authority. **Rule:** AGCL Point 01 moves taxation from income and labor to visible point-of-sale transactions. Point 05.2: a tax dollar assigned outside constitutional limits stays under the taxpayer's authority. Control 00A: taxing power, as fiscal authority, expires unless renewed. **Mechanism:** tax only at a visible transaction, every dollar traced on a public ledger, unauthorized spending redirected automatically, and taxing authority on a timer. **Alternative:** income and payroll taxes with yearly appropriations; rejected because the tax is hidden, follows the earner, and continues without proof. **Check:** the tax is shown at every purchase, every dollar can be traced, and no dollar is spent outside enumerated authority. |
| Political theology | Romans 13:6, "For this cause pay ye tribute also: for they are God's ministers" — tribute is consideration for the service of justice, including fair and just markets (**Principle**). Matthew 22:21 and Romans 13:7 — taxes are owed to governing authorities (**Command**; the counter-text to any claim that all taxation is wrong). Paul wrote under Rome, which taxed land and persons; that Romans does not by itself forbid a land tax is a counter-text the argument must meet through whole-system citation (rule 11) and F2. 1 Samuel 8:10–18 — the king's tenth is a warning of the cost of rejecting God, not an approval (**Precedent**, cautionary). 1 Kings 12 — Israel asked Rehoboam to lighten the yoke; he refused and consent was withdrawn (**Precedent**). Witnesses: Gibbon, *Decline and Fall*, ch. 6, on Roman customs: "in whatsoever manner the law was expressed, it was the Roman purchaser, and not the provincial merchant, who paid the tax"; Augustus's general excise was declared to support the army, a consumption tax with a stated purpose ([source](https://ccel.org/ccel/gibbon/decline/volume1/chap6.htm)). Caveat: Gibbon wrote of import duties; who bears a modern tax depends on how buyers and sellers adjust, so lessons cite Gibbon as a historical witness, not as economics. |

### F4 — Authority is delegated, and rulers can be corrected

| Lens | Entry |
|---|---|
| First principle | God defines all civil authority. Rulers serve under it, and there is a lawful way to bring them back into compliance. |
| Design rationale | **Problem:** rulers treat their power as their own and place themselves above the law that grants it. **Rule:** AGCL Control 000 (Scripture is the chief control; rulers are ministers, not owners); Control 00I (enforcement and remedy); Control 00J (amendment by explicit, published process). **Mechanism:** every authority traces to a higher source, and correction runs through law: flag, rule, amend. **Alternative:** authority that defines its own limits; rejected because nothing then stands above the ruler. **Check:** every authority names its source, and every correction is recorded. |
| Political theology | Romans 13:4 — the ruler is "the minister of God to thee for good," holding delegated authority (**Principle**). Acts 5:29 — "We ought to obey God rather than men" when a ruler commands what God forbids (**Command**). 2 Samuel 12 and 1 Kings 21 — prophets confront kings (**Precedent**). Acts 22:25–29 and 25:11 — Paul uses Rome's own law and appeals to Caesar, correcting officials through lawful process (**Precedent**). 1 Kings 12 — consent withdrawn from a king who would not lighten the yoke (**Precedent**). Witness: the Reformation doctrine of the lesser magistrate, which holds that lower officials must resist a higher ruler who breaks God's law. |

# 4. Edge Cases

| Case | Handling |
|---|---|
| A foundation conflicts with a CONTROL or AGCL document | The foundation is corrected; the control stands until a Ruling and amendment change it (§2) |
| An AGCL rule appears to conflict with Scripture | Flagged in writing; the Human Engineer rules; any change goes through AGCL Control 00J (§2) |
| A Scripture practice is cited without its conditions | Returned in review (rule 11) |
| A witness is cited as an authority | Returned in review (rule 12) |
| A reading of a passage is disputed | The lesson cites the Ruling, or marks the reading pending (rule 13) |
| A rule has no scriptural basis | Classified Modern design (rule 10) |
| A lesson says "Scripture commands" for an Analogy | Returned in review (rule 7) |
| A Scripture link omits a known counter-text | Returned in review (rule 8) |
| A claimed AGCL provision cannot be found in the canonical repository | Not taught as AGCL; recorded under "Not in the AGCL" |
| A reader rejects the theology lens | The first-principles and rationale lenses still stand on their own; no one is asked to profess faith (CONTROL 07) |
