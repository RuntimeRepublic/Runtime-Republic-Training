Every Saturday, the challenge gives you a short report. It tells you which encounter level you're working at, what new evidence came in that week, and what's left before your next level. Let's walk through a sample. Every number in it is made up.

Picture this. You started three weeks ago. You've been checking your money every few days. And this week, something arrives in the mail: your property tax notice.

Here's how your Saturday report reads.

Your level is CE1, the Sighting, and you're three quarters of the way to CE2. That's right. Three weeks of good work, and you still haven't earned CE2. Physical Evidence takes a full month, budgeted and closed. No shortcuts. The fourth week matters.

This week you reviewed forty-one transactions. Two didn't match anything. One was a thirty-eight dollar charge from a company you didn't recognize. The other was a transfer to savings that hasn't shown up on the other side yet. Neither one is a disaster. They're just open items, and the report lists them openly instead of pretending they're not there. You also spent a little over two hours on your money this week, and the report keeps track of that too.

Now the big news. The property tax notice.

Your notice says seven separate taxing bodies want a share of one house. The state. The county. The city. The school district. And three special districts. The report notes that the school district takes the biggest share, a little more than half. That's a fact, read straight from the notice. Then it tells you what it doesn't know yet: last year's notice. Without it, you can't tell whether your bill is growing faster than your income.

Seven governments, identified with evidence. That counts toward CE3, Contact. You haven't earned CE3 yet, because you haven't earned CE2. But the work is banked. Nothing you do is wasted.

The report also looks ahead. Your first half payment isn't due inside the next fourteen days, so it shows up in your thirty-day outlook instead. And the report says clearly that nothing has been scheduled or paid. That decision is yours.

Then there's your civic contact. Ten days ago, you wrote your congressman with a specific, reasonable request. He wrote back, but without much detail.

The report logs that contact, and then it tells you something useful. Congress doesn't set your property tax. The seven bodies on your notice do. So your letter counts as a civic action, but it doesn't count toward CE4, Communication. CE4 means contacting an official who sets your taxes. And the report suggests where to start: the school board, since it takes the biggest share.

Finally, the checklist for CE2. Three weeks of budgeting: done. Week four: not yet. Close and reconcile the month: not yet. List the open items: two so far. And then the reviews, first by AI, then by a person on another team.

That's the whole report. Your level, your evidence, what you don't know, and your next step. No guessing, no shortcuts, and no money moved without you.

## Recap

Let's sum up. The Saturday report shows your level, the week's new evidence, the open items, and what's left before your next level. In the sample, three weeks of work still leaves you at CE1, because CE2 takes a full month. A property tax notice naming seven governments counts toward CE3 and is banked. A letter to Congress is logged, but CE4 needs contact with the officials who set your taxes, like the school board. The report never pays or schedules anything. Thanks for listening.
