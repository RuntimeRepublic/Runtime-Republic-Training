Most organizations make joining easy and leaving hard. Runtime Republic does the opposite. Joining takes some work. Staying free is built in.

Everyone comes in through one of two doors.

Door A is for a new team: a few people starting from scratch. Door B is for an existing group: a Facebook group, a coalition, a club, or an issue team like FairTax. Either way, one person opens a team request, and the work begins.

If you lead an existing group, here's what you need to know first. You keep what you have. Your group keeps its name, its platform and its leaders. Your Runtime Republic team is a separate team with its own public repository. Neither one speaks for the other.

And nobody joins as a bloc. A leader can't sign up the members. Each person reads the six values, adopts them, and joins on their own. No chain of command carries over. The team picks its two admins from members who have done the work.

Why so careful? Because liberty is the art of elite capture avoidance. These rules keep any group from capturing its members, keep any group from capturing Runtime Republic, and keep Runtime Republic from capturing your group.

Then come the six gates, in order.

Gate 0 is the request. You open an issue on GitHub and say what your team is for, which door you're using, what level you work at (nation, state, county or city), and who you'd like as a sponsor.

Gate 1 is values. At least two founding members each build a private master plan and sign the six values in their own repository.

Gate 2 is purpose and sponsor. Your purpose is checked against the three frameworks, and the registry is checked for teams with the same objective so you can find them and work together. Teams can share the same objective; a match never blocks your team. Your sponsor accepts. You choose your team name and your two admins.

Gate 3 is standing up. You make a public team repository with a member list, a counts file and a folder for weekly packets, and you add your team to the registry.

Gate 4 is proving the loop. Every founding member passes the onboarding test and sets up two AI projects, one for personal finance and one for the team.

Gate 5 is your first closed week. At least two members file packets for the same week, and the team posts its first counts. That's when your team appears on the dashboard and changes from Forming to Active.

There are no time limits. You move to the next gate when the current one is checked, however long that takes. And if your team misses a week, it isn't over. It's paused, and it's active again the next week it posts.

That's the whole system. Two doors, six gates, and no one joins as a bloc.

Open your team request at runtimerepublic.com/onboard.
