Remember the guy in the movie who couldn't stop sculpting a mountain out of his mashed potatoes? He knew something was out there. He just couldn't explain it, and everyone at the dinner table thought he'd lost his mind.

Most of us have the same feeling every payday. We look at the check, then at what actually landed in the bank, and something is missing. We know it was taken. We just can't say who took it, how many of them there were, or where it went.

Here's the twist. It isn't little gray visitors from another galaxy. It's government. Not one government, either. Federal, state, county, city, school district, and a handful of special districts most people have never heard of. They've been landing on our paychecks, our utility bills, and our homes for years.

They're not visitors. They live here. And we pay their rent.

## The serious part

The jokes are fun, but the money is real. Taxes come out of your paycheck before your family sees a dime. Sales tax rides along on almost everything you buy. Fees and taxes hide in small print on your phone, power, and water bills. Property tax arrives once a year, and if you can't pay it, you can lose your home.

Most families can tell you what they spend on groceries. Very few can tell you how many governments take money from their household, or how much all of them take together.

That's a problem, because the only information most of us get about government spending comes from campaign promises. Promises tell you what politicians say they'll do with your money. An encounter tells you what was actually taken. One is a sales pitch. The other is evidence.

## The five encounters

We track progress the way the movie did, by how much evidence you have.

Encounter one is the Sighting. You know something is taking your money, but you don't track it.

Encounter two is Physical Evidence. You've run a real budget for a full month and closed it. You have receipts.

Encounter three is Contact. You've identified every government that takes your money, including the taxes hidden on your bills.

Encounter four is Communication. You've contacted the officials who actually set your taxes, and you kept a record.

Encounter five is First Contact Initiated. Your records meet the standards of the Runtime Republic frameworks. You're not waiting for them to contact you. You're setting the terms.

Every level is earned with evidence and checked by a reviewer. No claiming it. You have to show it.

## Who gets paid first?

This is the heart of the challenge. Ask most people what comes first in their budget, and they'll say family. Food, shelter, keeping the lights on, getting to work, saving for tomorrow. That's the order on paper, the one we'd choose.

Now look at how the money actually moves. Withholding is taken before your paycheck reaches you. Property tax has to be paid, or the house is at risk. Bill taxes are added before you can pay for the service itself. In practice, government often gets paid first, and the family gets what's left.

So here's the question. What is your real budget order? Not the order you'd like, and not the order a candidate promised. The order your own records show.

Once you can see it, you can decide whether it matches your values, and you can hold elected officials to evidence instead of promises.

## The challenge

Step one. Gather your evidence. Find one pay stub, one utility or phone bill, and one tax notice.

Step two. Count the governments. Write down every government named on those three documents. Most people are surprised.

Step three. Rank your budget order. List your top five spending categories by what you actually pay, and mark where government lands.

Step four. Post your encounter. Share your encounter level, your government count, and where government ranked, with the hashtag Close Encounter Challenge.

Step five. Tag three people, and challenge them to find out who's landing on their house.

Here's an example post. "Encounter one, working on encounter two. Counted seven governments on three documents. Government ranked number two in my real budget order, right behind the mortgage. Who's landing on your house?"

## Ground rules

Never post dollar amounts, addresses, account numbers, or photos of your bills. Share counts and rankings only.

Be fair. Taxes pay for roads, schools, courts, and first responders. This challenge isn't about having no government. It's about knowing exactly what you pay, who receives it, and judging them by evidence.

Be specific when you contact officials. Contact the bodies that actually set your taxes. A property tax question goes to your county, city, or school board, not to Congress.

## Phone home

The movie ended with ordinary people walking toward the lights, finally getting answers.

You don't need a mountain made of mashed potatoes. You need a pay stub, a bill, and a tax notice. Find out who's taking your money. Then decide who comes first in your house.

## Recap

Let's sum up. Governments, not aliens, are landing on our paychecks, our bills, and our homes. Campaign promises tell us what they say; our own records tell us what they took. The five encounters run from Sighting to First Contact Initiated, and each one is earned with evidence. The challenge is simple: gather three documents, count the governments, rank your real budget order, post your encounter without dollar amounts, and tag three people. Then decide who comes first in your house. Thanks for listening. This has been Runtime Republic.
