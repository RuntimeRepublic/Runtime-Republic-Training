The Civic Encounters Challenge takes five steps, and you can start tonight.

Step one. Gather your evidence. Find one pay stub, one utility or phone bill, and one tax notice. That's all you need. You probably have all three in a drawer or an email inbox right now.

Step two. Count the governments. Write down every government named on those three documents. Federal, state, county, city, school district, special districts. Most people are surprised by the number. Five is common. Seven or more is not unusual.

Step three. Rank your budget order. List your top five spending categories by what you actually pay, and mark where government lands.

Step four. Post your encounter. Share your encounter level, your government count, and where government ranked. Use the hashtag Civic Encounters Challenge.

Step five. Tag three people, and challenge them to find out who's landing on their house.

Here's an example post. "Encounter one, working on encounter two. Counted seven governments on three documents. Government ranked number two in my real budget order, right behind the mortgage. Who's landing on your house?"

Now the ground rules.

Never post dollar amounts, addresses, account numbers or photos of your bills. Share counts and rankings only. Your privacy matters more than any post. The count tells the story. The dollars don't need to.

Be fair. Taxes pay for roads, schools, courts and first responders. This challenge isn't about having no government. It's about knowing exactly what you pay, who receives it, and judging the results.

Be specific when you contact officials. A property tax question goes to your county, city or school board, not to Congress.

Milton Friedman warned that it's a great mistake "to judge policies and programs by their intentions rather than their results." Three documents and five steps are how you start judging results for yourself. Start tonight, and tell three friends tomorrow.

## Recap

Let's sum up. Gather three documents, count the governments, rank your real budget order, post your encounter with the hashtag Civic Encounters Challenge, and tag three people. Never post dollar amounts or personal details. Be fair, be specific, and judge by results. Want the full story? Listen to "The Civic Encounters Challenge" in the Civic Encounters series.
