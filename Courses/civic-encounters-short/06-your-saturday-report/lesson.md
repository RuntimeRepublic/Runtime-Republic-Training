Every Saturday, the challenge gives you a short report. Here's a sample from week three. Every number in it is made up.

Your level is CE1, the Sighting, and you're three quarters of the way to CE2. Three weeks of good work, and you still haven't earned it. Physical Evidence takes a full month, budgeted and closed. No shortcuts.

This week you reviewed forty-one transactions. Two didn't match anything: a small charge from a company you didn't recognize, and a transfer to savings that hasn't shown up yet. The report lists them openly. A report that hides its open items is a report you can't trust.

Then the big news. Your property tax notice arrived. Seven separate taxing bodies want a share of one house, and the school district takes a little more than half. That's a fact, read straight from the notice. The report also says what it doesn't know yet: last year's notice, which you'd need to see whether your bill is growing faster than your income.

Seven governments, identified with evidence. That counts toward CE3, Contact. You haven't earned it yet, but the work is banked.

The report looks ahead too. Your first payment isn't due in the next two weeks, so it shows up in your thirty-day outlook. And nothing has been scheduled or paid. That decision is yours. Thomas Sowell said, "There are no solutions. There are only trade-offs." The report makes sure you see every trade-off before you choose.

Finally, your civic contact. You wrote your congressman, and he replied without much detail. The report logs it, then tells you something useful. Congress doesn't set your property tax. The seven bodies on your notice do. So CE4 means contacting one of them. Start with the school board.

Your level, your evidence, what you don't know, and your next step. Every Saturday.

## Recap

Let's sum up. The Saturday report shows your level, the week's evidence, open items and your next step. A full month is required for CE2. A tax notice counts toward CE3 and is banked. CE4 means contacting the officials who set your taxes. Thomas Sowell reminds us there are only trade-offs. Want the full story?
