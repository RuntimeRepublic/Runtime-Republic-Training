Every big idea has critics. Fair is fair, so here are their strongest points about H.R. 25, the FairTax Act, and the answers.

Critics say a twenty-three percent sales tax will feel like sticker shock on every purchase. The answer: the shock is the point. Today's federal tax hides in withholding and in prices, so families can't add it up at all. A tax you can see is a tax you can judge.

Critics say a high sales tax invites cheating, with cash deals under the table. The answer: today's system already leaks. The IRS projected a gap of about six hundred ninety-six billion dollars between what was owed and what was paid for tax year 2022. Both systems have cheaters. The fair question is which one has fewer.

Critics say asking fifty states to collect a federal tax is a burden. The answer: Americans already spend about six point nine billion hours a year on federal tax paperwork, and most states already run sales taxes.

Critics say the burden could shift toward middle-income families. That one is a fair question, and it's still open. It needs a careful side-by-side comparison with today's taxes.

And the strongest criticism of all. The bill doesn't actually repeal the Sixteenth Amendment. It only says Congress finds that it should be repealed. So a future Congress could bring back an income tax on top of the sales tax. Two taxes instead of one.

Milton Friedman warned, "Nothing is so permanent as a temporary government program." Promises made by one Congress can be undone by the next. The only full answer is a constitutional amendment, a rule the next majority can't quietly change.

You can't leave a bad system and wait for a perfect one. But you can move to an imperfect one that sends clear signals and has a path forward.

## Recap

Let's sum up. Critics raise sticker shock, cheating, state burden, and who pays more. Most of those problems exist today too, but who pays more is still open. The strongest risk is an income tax returning without a Sixteenth Amendment repeal, and Milton Friedman reminds us that nothing is so permanent as a temporary government program. Want the full story?
