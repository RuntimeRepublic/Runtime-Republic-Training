Here's a thought experiment. Suppose Congress passed one bill, H.R. 25, the FairTax Act, and nothing else changed.

As introduced in January 2025, H.R. 25 repeals the federal income tax, payroll taxes, and the estate and gift taxes. It replaces them with one national sales tax of twenty-three percent, starting in 2027. It abolishes the IRS. The states collect the tax and keep a small fee. And every registered household gets a monthly payment that covers the tax on spending up to the poverty level.

The American Gold Contract for Liberty calls passing H.R. 25 the first step to ending the tax trap. So how far does that step take us?

At home, a lot changes. The federal tax leaves your paycheck and shows up on every receipt instead. A month of receipts proves exactly what you paid. Encounter two gets easy. The federal part of encounter three is nearly automatic.

That's the power of a visible tax. Adam Smith wrote in 1776, "The tax which each individual is bound to pay ought to be certain, and not arbitrary." A tax on every receipt is about as certain as it gets.

But your property tax notice doesn't change at all. Neither do the fees on your phone and power bills. So for state and local government, most families would still be at encounter one or two.

Nationally, everyone sees the federal tax at the register. But encounter three only gets halfway. You'd know exactly what Washington takes. You still wouldn't know where it goes, because the bill doesn't trace spending.

So the honest answer is this. H.R. 25 alone is a big win for seeing what's taken. It's the first step, and only the first step. The rest of the plan is what carries us to encounter five.

## Recap

Let's sum up. If only H.R. 25 passed, federal taxes would leave the paycheck and show up on every receipt. Adam Smith said a tax should be certain, and this one would be. But property tax and bill fees wouldn't change, and spending still wouldn't be traced. It's the first step, not the last. Want the full story?
