Remember the guy in the movie who couldn't stop sculpting a mountain out of his mashed potatoes? He knew something was out there. He just couldn't explain it.

Most of us feel the same way every payday. We look at the check, then at what landed in the bank, and something is missing. We know it was taken. We just can't say who took it, how many of them there were, or where it went.

Here's the twist. It isn't little gray visitors from another galaxy. It's government. And not just one. Federal, state, county, city, school district, and a handful of special districts most people have never heard of. They've been landing on our paychecks, our utility bills and our homes for years.

They're not visitors. They live here. And we pay their rent.

Nobody hands you a single bill for all of it. It comes in pieces, from different offices, on different days. That's why it's so hard to see the total.

The jokes are fun, but the money is real. Taxes come out of your paycheck before your family sees a dime. Sales tax rides along on almost everything you buy. Fees and taxes hide in the small print on your phone, power and water bills. Property tax arrives once a year, and if you can't pay it, you can lose your home.

Most families can tell you what they spend on groceries. Very few can tell you how many governments take money from their household, or how much all of them take together.

That matters, because most of what we hear about government spending comes from campaign promises. Promises tell you what politicians say they'll do with your money. Your own records tell you what was actually taken. One is a sales pitch. The other is evidence.

Milton Friedman warned that it's a great mistake "to judge policies and programs by their intentions rather than their results." The Civic Encounters Challenge is how you start judging by results, beginning with your own kitchen table. All it takes to start is three documents and a little curiosity.

## Recap

Let's sum up. Governments, not aliens, are landing on our paychecks, our bills and our homes. Campaign promises tell us what they say. Our own records tell us what they took. Milton Friedman reminds us to judge by results, not intentions. Want the full story? Listen to "The Civic Encounters Challenge" in the Civic Encounters series. Thanks for listening.
