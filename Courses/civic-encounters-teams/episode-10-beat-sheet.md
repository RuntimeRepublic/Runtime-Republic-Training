## Beats
0. **Opening: the dashboard (from the office).** A letter's QR code sends Nora, the staffer, to the public response board. She expects a protest site. Instead: flags lit across five countries, state tiles across dozens of states, a news bar of teams forming and letters mailed, and a list of offices with statuses: Asked, Responded, Committed, Delivered. Her own office is on it: "Asked." Counts only, no names, no dollar amounts. Then the news bar flashes, bigger than the rest: "BREAKING: The Plain Price Coalition joins Runtime Republic, all chapters, all 50 states." A national group that existed long before any app, now adopting the same standard in one vote. The counts jump while she watches. She leans back. "How long has this been going on?"
1. **Monday mail.** Nora sorts the week's letters into the usual bins: taxes, roads, housing,
   small business. Most mail is a single issue. These look like it too.
2. **The odd details.** A gas-tax letter asks where the federal fuel tax goes after the pump,
   step by step. A renter's letter asks what her state would give up and gain under a
   33⅓ / 33⅓ / 33⅓ revenue split. A landlord sends compliance hours, not complaints. A letter
   rates H.R. 25 and suggests fixes: a smoke test at the end and a 4-month review.
   Each includes a stamped reply card.
3. **The click.** Different topics, different towns, but every letter cites the same sections
   of the same standard (AGCL and NBBF) and makes the same ask: join the project and work in
   the open. They aren't separate complaints. They're pieces of one plan.
4. **Laying it out.** Nora spreads the letters across a conference table in the order of the
   plan: the trace, revenue sharing, telemetry, clawback, bill standards, House rules.
   Seen together, it reads like a blueprint, written by kitchen-table teams, not lobbyists.
5. **What's different.** There's no march and no outrage campaign. It's evidence, a standard
   and a steady stream from teams that keep splitting and growing. Nora realizes the mail
   won't stop after one news cycle; it is designed to keep coming, week after week.
6. **One economist, by name, where AGCL supports the point** (series checklist E3), chosen
   from QUOTE-LOG.md, on testing whether laws do what they promise.
7. **The Member.** Nora brings the pile to Representative Hale and explains what she sees.
   Hale asks the honest question: "What happens if we say yes?" Nora: "Then we work in the open.
   Same as them."
8. **Close.** Nora picks up one reply card and checks the box: "Our office will participate."
   She signs it, puts it in the outgoing mail and the story ends: politics won't be the same.
