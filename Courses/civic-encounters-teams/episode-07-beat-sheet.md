## Beats
0. **Opening: the dashboard (Mike).** Mike opens with news: a flag lit up. The gray Canada flag turned to full color: the first team outside the U.S., started by someone who read their story. The members count on their own card says 8, and Mike grins: "The dashboard's telling us something else too. We're too big for one table."
1. **Eight people, one call.** The meeting runs long. Not everyone gets to talk. Gary can barely hear on his phone.
2. **Mike raises the rule.** Past seven, teams split instead of growing into one big group.
3. **Pushback.** Linda doesn't want to lose people. Mike: splitting isn't losing; it's how the idea spreads, like the 1770s committees of correspondence.
4. **The split.** Two consumer teams form. Walt starts a business team of rental owners, and stays on a consumer team too (rule 2).
5. **How they stay connected.** Each team has its own GitHub repo and AI project; Runtime Republic keeps one registry; evidence and records are shared. The dashboard counts both teams.
6. **Gary gives in.** To see the dashboard and the shared records, he buys a laptop (rule 7 payoff). Mike helps him set up his AI project, with security first.
7. **One economist, by name, where AGCL supports the point**: knowledge spread across many people works better than one central planner.
8. **Close.** Gary's first look at the dashboard: two teams, one more flag lit. He grins: "The aliens are outnumbered."

## Recap
- Teams split after seven to keep meetings working and spread the idea.
- Business teams are welcome; every member stays on a consumer team.
- GitHub, AI and Runtime Republic connect the teams.
