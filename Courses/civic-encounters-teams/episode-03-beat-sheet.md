James decided (Nov 15): the turning point is both. Walt is squeezed too and becomes an ally, and Peggy's estimate is the key evidence.

## Beats
1. **Surprise guests.** Linda asked. Walt, her landlord, didn't just answer; he showed up to the next online meeting with Peggy, his bookkeeper. The line "ending property taxes" got his attention.
2. **Walt on guard.** He expects to be blamed. He owns a few rental houses. He sets the record straight: rent pays the mortgage, insurance, repairs, and the tax bill, which keeps rising whether he likes it or not.
3. **Peggy's numbers.** She shares a rough estimate (fictional, shown as a share, not dollars): about one month's rent in twelve goes to property tax on Linda's house. Key evidence, labeled an estimate.
4. **The room changes.** Linda realizes Walt isn't the one taking her money; he's passing it through. Walt realizes the renters are paying his tax bill and getting blamed for "high rent" alongside him. Same side.
5. **Peggy's second point.** Compliance time: hours each year on tax paperwork for a small rental business. That's waste, in Lean terms (series study idea).
6. **The rule.** Walt wants a team for landlords. Mike explains the rule: everyone in a business team also belongs to a consumer team, so no one ends up on opposite sides (series rule 2). Walt stays.
7. **One economist, by name, where AGCL supports the point**: who actually bears a tax is not always who writes the check.
8. **Close.** Five members: a renter, an owner, someone who knows the books, and two friends. Peggy: "Next week, let's line up what each of us really pays first."

## Recap
- A tax can pass through several hands before anyone notices it.
- Estimates are evidence when they're labeled as estimates.
- Business members stay on a consumer team.
