## Beats

### Part A — The time-lapse (lead-in; no economist quote, per series rule E3)

**How Part A works.** The narration cuts back and forth: a passage of the speech, then what
Mike is doing at the same moment, then back to the speech. Name rule: the speaker is called
only "Reagan" (last name), never a first name or title.

**Quote rule (James, Nov 16, 2026).** Each speech quote runs about 20 to 25 words, roughly a
full sentence or two. Every quote is copied word for word from the Ronald Reagan Presidential
Library transcript, never from memory or a secondary site. The anchor phrase given in each beat
is verified; the writer extends it to the full sentence around it in the Library text.

1. **Narrator introduces the speech (when and why).** On October 27, 1964, one week before the
   presidential election, a former actor named Reagan went on national television to speak for
   Barry Goldwater, the Republican candidate, who was expected to lose badly. The pre-recorded
   half-hour program was called *Rendezvous with Destiny*. The speech was "A Time for Choosing."
   People who heard it called it simply "The Speech," and it launched Reagan's political career.
2. **Cut to Mike.** Sixty-two years later, a Saturday morning. Mike's kitchen table: coffee, a
   shoebox of receipts, his iPhone propped against the sugar bowl, his laptop open to his AI
   budget. The screen door is open to the porch. He finds the speech in a podcast on Spotify and
   taps play. The narration speeds up: time-lapse.
3. **Speech (quote, 20–25 words).** Reagan opens by saying he spent most of his life as a
   Democrat and now follows another course. Anchor: "I have spent most of my life as a Democrat."
   **Cut to Mike:** he logs his **pay stub**. Gross pay at the top, take-home at the bottom, and
   between them the lines most people skip: federal withholding and Kansas withholding. The
   government was paid before Mike was.
4. **Speech (quote, 20–25 words).** Reagan says the choice isn't between left and right.
   Anchor: "no left or right, only an up or down." Extend to his full contrast: up toward
   individual freedom, down toward total government control.
   **Cut to Mike:** he logs a **grocery receipt**. The AI flags the sales tax: Kansas dropped its
   state sales tax on groceries, but local sales tax is still on the line. One government moved;
   the others didn't. (Verify both facts in SOURCES.md.)
5. **Speech (quote, 20–25 words).** Reagan on urban renewal: officials can declare a property
   substandard and owners lose control of it. Anchor: "Private property rights have become so
   diluted."
   **Cut to Mike:** he logs his **mortgage escrow statement**. Part of every payment goes to
   property tax, split among the county, the city, the school district and others he has to
   look up. He writes in the margin: "Who are all of these?"
6. **Speech (quote, 20–25 words).** Reagan on Social Security, presented as his claim, not a
   fact the essay endorses. Anchor: "it is not insurance but is a welfare program."
   **Cut to Mike:** back to the pay stub. He logs the **FICA lines**: Social Security 6.2% and
   Medicare 1.45%, taken before payday.
7. **Speech (quote, 20–25 words).** Reagan on the tax burden. Anchors: "thirty-seven cents of
   every dollar earned" and "one-third of its national income." The narrator says plainly these
   are Reagan's 1964 figures, not today's.
   **Cut to Mike:** he logs his **electric bill**. The AI splits it: energy, a franchise fee,
   sales tax, and a provider surcharge marked "not government unless proven." (Series rule:
   never call a provider surcharge a tax without evidence.)
8. **Speech (quote, 20–25 words).** Reagan on government agencies that never shrink. Anchor:
   "nearest thing to eternal life."
   **Cut to Mike:** he logs his **phone bill**: a 911 fee, sales tax, and a federal Universal
   Service charge the carrier passes through. He smiles at the timing. (UNVERIFIED story to check
   before use: the federal telephone excise tax started in 1898 to pay for the Spanish-American
   War and was collected on long distance until 2006.)
9. **Speech (quote, 20–25 words).** Reagan relays a foreign official's remark about American
   debt. Anchor: "My country's debt is greater than the combined debt."
   **Cut to Mike:** he logs a **gas receipt**: 18.4 cents a gallon federal, 24 cents a gallon
   Kansas (K.S.A. 79-34,141), plus a small state fee, all hidden inside the pump price. Nobody
   sees them on the receipt. (Plants episode 8, the gas tax letter.)
10. **Speech (quote, 20–25 words).** Reagan's close. Anchor: "a rendezvous with destiny." Extend
    to his full closing sentence about preserving freedom for our children.
    **Cut to Mike:** the time-lapse slows to normal speed. His AI shows the week's tax lines in
    one column. He circles the total.

### Part B — Gary at the screen door

11. **A knock on the frame.** Gary is on the porch, back from a long week. Through the screen
    he hears the voice before he sees Mike. "Is that Reagan? My dad played that on vinyl."
12. **Mike pauses Spotify** on his iPhone and waves him in. "Coffee's on. Sit down."
13. **Transition: both at the table.** Gary sees the shoebox, the circled number, and the
    column of tax lines. Payday was three days ago and his account is already thin. "I don't
    even know where mine goes." Mike turns the laptop toward him.

### Part C — The talk about taxes

14. **Mike agrees it feels that way.** He doesn't laugh. A year ago he felt the same.
15. **Mike's story: the power of AI (James, Nov 14).** The first month he ran his budget with
    AI, it found subscriptions and services he had lost track of. He cancelled them.
16. **The turn.** Taxes are the same. Most of us lost track of them a long time ago: taken
    before the paycheck lands, added on top of bills, folded into rent and prices. Mike points
    at the column: "Every one of these was in the speech this morning."
17. **The CE1-to-CE2 scale (the series' only alien reference).** Mike: "The challenge borrows
    its levels from UFO sightings. CE1 is the Sighting: you know something is taking your money,
    but you can't prove it. CE2 is Physical Evidence: one month, every receipt, every tax."
    Gary: "So I'm a Sighting." Mike: "So was I." No more aliens after this beat, in any episode.
18. **Up, not left or right.** Gary asks if this is a Republican thing, because of the speech.
    Mike: "Reagan said it himself: not left or right, up or down. The levels go up. Anybody can
    climb them." (Ties to the series purpose: no opposing sides, everyone works it out together.)
19. **The challenge.** Track your money for one month and find every tax. Not just income tax:
    every line on every bill, receipt and pay stub.
20. **Gary's objection.** No laptop. His bills come by mail (rule 7). Mike: "Start with what
    you've got. Keep the envelopes. We'll meet online next week; you can join by phone."
21. **One economist, by name, where AGCL supports the point** (series checklist E3): a tax you
    can't see is a tax you can't judge. Quote chosen from QUOTE-LOG.md, by name only, and only
    if the AGCL documents support it (CONTROL 09 rule 14).
22. **Close.** Gary: "So what's my time for choosing?" Mike: "Tonight. One month." They shake on
    it: next week, same time, Gary brings his first three documents (a pay stub, a utility bill,
    a receipt). Mike: "Bring stamps too." Gary: "Why?" Mike: "You'll see." (Plants rule 8; paid
    off in episode 8, Talking Back.)

## Recap (CONTROL 05 style, for the lesson end)
- Most people lost track of their taxes the way they lose track of subscriptions.
- One ordinary week of receipts holds taxes from several governments, most of them hidden.
- CE1 is the Sighting; CE2 is Physical Evidence. The challenge: one month, find every tax.
