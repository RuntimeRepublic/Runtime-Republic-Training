The American Gold Contract for Liberty, the AGCL, rests on one governing rule. Government authority must be defined, limited, transparent, measurable and reversible. If any one of those is missing, the system is invalid.

For years that sounded like a slogan. In an agentic runtime, it becomes a test that actually runs.

The AGCL has ten Control Documents, numbered 00A through 00J. Read them today and they sound almost like requirements written for this moment. Here's how each one comes to life by June of 2027.

Control 00A is Authority and Expiration. It says no authority may exist by inertia. In the runtime, every campaign is a milestone with a due date. If a task sits open with no owner, the weekly check flags it. Nothing drifts along just because nobody stopped it.

Control 00B is Signal and Non-Interference. It says the truth must reach decision-makers without being filtered. In the runtime, the weekly check posts the raw numbers first. Commentary comes second, and it's labeled as commentary.

Control 00C is Property Rights and Pooling. It says ownership comes before government. In the runtime, the foreclosure tracker shows, home by home, where property rights are being lost to tax bills.

Control 00D is Exit and Disengagement. It says exit is a right, not a favor. In the runtime, any county chapter can copy the plan, change it and leave with its full history. Nobody is trapped.

Control 00E is Debt and Intergenerational Liability. It says debt must have a defined end. In the runtime, the Guide builds a first draft of a fiscal note in minutes, showing who pays, how much and for how long. People check the math, and the math stays in the open.

Control 00F is Uncertainty and Black-Swan Stress. It says every system must declare how it can fail. In the runtime, every plan lists its unknowns, and anything that hasn't been confirmed is labeled unverified.

Control 00G is Transparency and Public Ledger. It says hidden authority is illegitimate authority. In the runtime, a public repository is a public ledger by default.

Control 00H is Constitutional Compatibility. It says every power must trace back to the Constitution. In the runtime, every bill draft has to cite its authority, and the Guide refuses to fill a gap by making something up.

Control 00I is Enforcement and Remedy. It says breaking the rules must carry a consequence, automatically. In the runtime, checks fail out loud. Labels change. Scorecards update on their own, so nobody can choose to look the other way.

And Control 00J is Amendment and Version Control. It says change must be explicit and recorded. In the runtime, that's simply what GitHub is. Every change has an author, a date and a reason.

That last one is the heart of it. The AGCL says a government that changes without a record can't stay free. Git, the engine underneath GitHub, is a version-control system. The principle and the tool were made for each other. They just hadn't met yet.

Now they have. And when a citizen opens a Claude Project to check a bill, the Guide doesn't measure it against someone's opinion. It measures it against ten written controls that anyone can read.
